## Claim boundary

> RGCS is a coordinate, mapping, signal, and provenance research workbench. It converts
> received or user-supplied numeric vectors into reproducible binary/octal parse
> receipts, candidate Earth-root positions, great-circle paths, and polygonal map
> regions.
>
> RGCS does not claim that anomalous sources, craft, crop formations, physical
> propulsion, or non-human communication are proven.

```text
Vertex POSITIONS are projector output and remain underdetermined under V1-B01/B02.
Path, polygon, distance, perimeter, centroid, and area geometry are exact for the
selected vertices.

The tool verifies geometry.
It does not verify that a candidate vertex is physically true.
```

## Features

**Codec lane**
- Direct 9-digit words parsed by binary/octal path — never as decimal triplets.
- Two exact, reversible field cuts: `F5|Q22|S3` and `R4|S8|P12|tail`.
- Staged variable grammar with floating boundaries; field labels are maximum
  envelopes, not always-full fields.
- `S3` / M3 kept out of the geometry, asserted behaviourally.
- Octal branch partition `117` (Britain) / `120` (North America).
- Wide-envelope records (34–41 bits) refused, **never truncated**.

**Frame and receipts**
- `FrameManifest` and `AddressCertificate`: Earth root D_V1, epoch gating, claim class
  and applicable blockers travel with every coordinate. A bare lat/lon is never
  emitted.
- Exports: GeoJSON, KML, CSV, and a full JSON receipt bundle.

**Map workbench**
- `parse`, `map`, `path`, `polygon`, `serve` — runnable without reading the rest of
  the repository.
- Two-vector great-circle paths: 128-sample slerp polyline, distance, initial/final
  bearing, midpoint.
- N-vector polygons: area, perimeter, centroid, per-edge distances, branch mix,
  self-intersection detection.
- **Interactive polygon builder**: text entry (single or comma-separated), Add,
  Remove per row, Up to reorder, "Order by bearing", "Clear all", preset chips. Live
  recompute on every edit.
- Leaflet vendored locally; pages need no CDN.

## How the geometry is checked

| property | check |
|---|---|
| Great-circle distance | haversine, spherical law of cosines, and Vincenty-on-sphere agree to < 1e-6 km; 90° separation is exactly ¼ circumference |
| Path is a real great circle | sampled midpoint equidistant from both ends; segment lengths sum to the reported distance |
| Polygon area | L'Huilier fan **and** Gauss–Bonnet turning angles agree to ~1e-13; a spherical octant is exactly ⅛ of the sphere; a spherical triangle exceeds its planar Heron area |
| Browser kernel | the real page JavaScript is run and compared to the Python library: **0.000000 m** drift across all seven known vectors |
| Codec | PATH7 decompositions computed arithmetically in test, not asserted in prose |

**A formula bug was found and fixed during this work.** The first area cross-check used
the planar "spherical shoelace" approximation; anchored against closed forms it was
wrong by a factor of two on a spherical octant and by 42 % on the three-anchor triangle
(513,257 vs the correct 299,098 km²). It was removed rather than kept as a weak second
opinion, and the tests are now anchored to closed forms rather than to
previously-recorded output.

Numerical conditioning is recorded rather than smoothed: the fan triangulation
privileges vertex 0, so rotating a thin sliver's vertex list moves the area by 3.8e-9
relative — under a square metre on 105 km². The turning method is exactly
rotation-invariant. Both facts are asserted.

## What changed since the previous README

The prior public README presented RGCS primarily as a resonance/FEM research framework
and did not describe a map workbench at all. It has been archived to
[`docs/archive/pre-r1059/`](../archive/pre-r1059/) with a banner naming four superseded
assumptions:

- `165879243` labelled as Montréal — retired to hint/provenance;
- the header-stripped affine transport bridge described as confirmed — refuted;
- fixed-field `R4|S8|P12|tail` treated as *the* grammar — demoted to a diagnostic view;
- nine-digit words read as `16 | payload | 3` — the leading `16` is a rendering
  artifact.

---

## What remains unproven

Everything about **where** the vectors point.

The parse is exact. The frame is declared. The geometry is cross-checked. But the
projector `A` retains two free parameters at three anchors, and two equally admissible
members of that family place `165879243` on different continents. Until that is
resolved, every projected point is a candidate.

The 15 km cell-scale relation is suggestive at ±5 % (coincidence rate 0.147) and
remains a single observation. The water-acceptance criterion — the only check that
needs no labelled anchor — cannot run without a coastline dataset.
